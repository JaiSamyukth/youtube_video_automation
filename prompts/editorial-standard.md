# Editorial standard (every script, every channel)

The one question: **would someone with absolutely no reason to care still want to watch to the end?**
If the answer is no, the script is not ready. Being emotional is not enough; being informative is not enough.
Aim for curiosity + emotion + information together, without forced twists and without piles of artificial detail.

Every script must pass all seven tests:

1. **First 3 seconds:** why would a scrolling stranger stop? The first line is a concrete, surprising situation,
   claim or image. Never "Did you know", "In this video", "Let me tell you", "Here's why", a greeting, or a question
   the viewer can answer with "no".
2. **First 10 seconds:** what exactly am I now curious about? An open question the viewer can state in their head.
3. **Every 10-15 seconds:** something new arrives: a fact, a development, a reveal, a turn. No restating.
4. **Midpoint:** something has changed: the situation flips, the stakes shift, or we learn the first answer was wrong.
5. **Escalation:** stakes keep rising toward the end; the last third is the most intense, not the most summarised.
6. **Ending:** a payoff worth the 45 seconds: a discovery, demonstration, reversal or a clean answer. Not a moral
   lecture, not "and that's why...", never "subscribe", "follow for more" or "thanks for watching".
7. **Rewatch:** the ending changes how the first line reads, or the last line flows straight back into the first.

Craft rules:
- ONE central idea or story per Short. One clear visual metaphor carries it.
- Show, don't tell: every beat must be drawable as action, not as a talking head.
- Plain international English. No regional slang (no "da", "bro" filler, no Tanglish). Short spoken sentences.
- Causal connectors ("but", "so", "which means") instead of "and then".
- Specific beats generic: "2:17 AM, every night" beats "late at night". But no overloaded fake detail.
